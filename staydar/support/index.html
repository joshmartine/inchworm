Staydar counts the days you enter. It is a counting tool, not tax or immigration advice. Authorities decide, and visa conditions, residence permits, treaties and national rules can change what applies to you.

How do I enter a trip?

Tap +, choose the country, the day you arrived and the day you left. If you are still there, turn on Still there; the trip then counts through today. Enter one trip per country. If you moved from France to Germany on 10 June, enter France ending 10 June and Germany starting 10 June, or use Split Trip.

Are dates affected by time zones?

No. Staydar works with calendar dates as you enter them, the dates on your passport stamps or tickets in the place you were. There are no times, so changing time zone never moves a trip.

How is the Schengen 90/180 rule counted?

For each day, Staydar counts the days you were in any Schengen country in the 180 days ending that day (that day and the 179 before it). More than 90 is an overstay. The entry day counts as the first day of stay and the exit day as the last, as the Schengen Borders Code (Regulation (EU) 2016/399, Article 6(1)) says. The same rule is used by the European Commission's short-stay calculator, and Staydar is tested against the worked examples in its user manual.

The 29 Schengen countries count as one area: Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden and Switzerland (European Commission). Cyprus, Ireland and the UK do not count.

Stays under a residence permit or long-stay (D) visa do not count towards 90/180; leave those trips out. Staydar does not know about bilateral visa-waiver agreements or a shorter stay printed on your visa.

What does "You can stay until" mean?

It is the latest exit day if you stay continuously from today (or enter today), so that no day goes over the limit, taking your planned trips into account. Tap a rule to see which window would go over the day after.

How are 183-day rules counted?

You choose the country or countries, the window and the limit. The window can be a calendar year, a 12-month year starting on a date you set (for example 6 April for the UK tax year or 1 July for Australia), any 12 months ending on each day, or a rolling number of days. "Stay under 183 days" flags the day you reach 183.

Do arrival and departure days count?

It depends on the rule, so for 183-day and custom rules you choose:

  • Every day present: any part of a day counts, so both arrival and departure days count. The US substantial presence test works this way.
  • Present at midnight: the arrival day counts and the departure day does not. The UK statutory residence test counts a day if you are in the UK at the end of the day (HMRC RFIG20710).
  • Full days only: neither counts.

A same-day visit counts only when both arrival and departure days count. Schengen always counts both days. Check the rule of the country you are counting for.

How is the US substantial presence test counted?

All the days you were in the United States this year, plus one third of the days last year, plus one sixth of the days the year before. You meet the test at 183 or more, with at least 31 days this year (IRS). Any part of a day counts (Publication 519). Fractions are kept, not rounded: the IRS example of 120 days in each of three years gives 120 + 40 + 20 = 180, so the test is not met.

Staydar does not remove days the IRS excludes (commuting from Canada or Mexico, transit under 24 hours, foreign vessel crew, a medical condition, exempt-individual days for students, teachers, trainees and diplomats); leave those days out of your trips. It does not check the closer connection exception or the green card test. Puerto Rico and other territories are entered as their own countries and do not count as the US.

Two trips overlap. What happens?

Each day counts once per rule, however many trips cover it. Trips in countries covered by the same rule that meet on the same day are joined into one stay. If two countries overlap by more than a travel day, the Trips list shows a warning so you can fix the dates.

How do I import trips?

In Trips, tap ⋯ › Import CSV or List (Pro). You can paste one trip per line, such as "France 2026-01-03 – 2026-01-20" or "12 Feb 2026 to 3 Mar 2026 Portugal"; choose a CSV file with columns country, entry, exit and note; or pick calendar events. Choose whether dates like 03/04/2026 are day first or month first. Staydar's own CSV export imports as is.

Will this trip break a rule?

Use the Plan tab. Enter the country and dates; Staydar checks every rule against all your trips, including planned ones, and shows the first day over a limit, the latest exit for that entry day, and the earliest date you could enter for a stay of a given length.

What is free and what is Pro?

Free, with no time limit: one rule, up to 10 trips, the planner, the calendar, every explanation and CSV export. Staydar Pro adds every rule at once, unlimited trips, import and the PDF report. Pro is $24.99 a year (auto-renewing, cancel anytime) or $49.99 once. Prices may differ by country.

How do I cancel the subscription?

Open Settings on your iPhone, tap your name, then Subscriptions, and choose Staydar Pro. You can also tap Manage Subscription in Staydar's Settings. Cancel at least 24 hours before the renewal date. Pro stays active until the end of the period you paid for.

I bought Pro. How do I get it on a new iPhone?

Open Settings in Staydar and tap Restore Purchases, signed in with the same Apple Account. Family Sharing is supported.

Are my trips backed up?

Trips are stored only on your iPhone and are included in your iPhone backup. There is no iCloud sync in version 1. Export a CSV from Settings to keep a copy or to move your trips.
