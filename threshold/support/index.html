Are the results a loan approval?

No. Every result is an estimate for planning and discussion. It is not a loan approval, rate quote, Loan Estimate or lending decision. Lenders apply their own guidelines, credit review and current program limits.

How is the monthly payment calculated?

Principal and interest = L × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)−n), where L is the loan, i is the yearly rate ÷ 12 and n is the number of monthly payments. Property tax and homeowner's insurance can be a percent of the price or a yearly dollar amount, divided by 12. Mortgage insurance is a yearly percent of the loan, divided by 12. PITI adds these unrounded and rounds once; HOA dues are added for the total housing payment. Every screen shows these steps with your numbers under "How it's calculated".

How does qualifying work?

Housing expense is PITI plus HOA dues. The housing (front-end) ratio is housing expense ÷ gross monthly income; the total (back-end) ratio adds monthly debts. The maximum loan is the loan whose housing expense equals the smaller of income × housing ratio and income × total ratio − debts. Threshold also shows the larger "unrestricted" loan and the allowable debt that would make both ratios meet, and can find the income needed for a price or a buyer's actual ratios.

Where do the conventional, FHA and VA presets come from?

They are starting points you can edit:

  • Conventional 28% / 36%: the traditional guideline. Fannie Mae allows up to 36% total for manual underwriting, 45% with compensating factors and 50% through Desktop Underwriter (Selling Guide B3-6-02, April 2, 2025).
  • FHA 31% / 43%: HUD's limits for manually underwritten loans (Mortgagee Letter 2014-02). Annual MIP default 0.55%, from Mortgagee Letter 2023-05 (February 22, 2023): terms over 15 years, base loan up to $726,200 and LTV over 95%; 0.50% at 95% LTV or less. The 1.75% upfront premium is usually financed; add it to the loan amount.
  • VA 41% total, no housing ratio, plus residual income (38 CFR 36.4340). Threshold does not compute residual income.

Program limits change. The app does not include conforming or FHA county loan limits; check current agency guidance.

Why can totals differ by a few cents from a handheld calculator?

Settings › Rounding has two choices. Like Loan Documents (the default) rounds each month's interest and daily interest to the cent and uses a fixed rounded payment, with the last payment clearing the balance, as a Loan Estimate, Closing Disclosure or loan statement does. Like a Handheld Calculator carries interest unrounded and only rounds the answer, as handheld real estate calculators do. Money is always computed in exact decimal arithmetic and rounded half up to the cent.

How is APR calculated?

Amount financed = loan − points − other prepaid finance charges − prepaid interest. The APR is 12 × the monthly rate at which the monthly payments repay the amount financed (the actuarial method of Regulation Z, Appendix J). It leaves out mortgage insurance and odd first periods.

How are cash to close and the seller net sheet laid out?

Both follow the CFPB's sample Closing Disclosure (Regulation Z, Appendix H-25(B)): closing costs, minus costs paid before closing and costs financed, plus the down payment, minus the deposit, seller credits and other credits. With the sample's numbers the app gives its $9,712.10 closing costs, $14,147.26 cash to close and $64,414.96 cash to the seller.

What do refinance break-even and rent vs buy mean?

Break-even = closing costs ÷ monthly payment savings, in months; the app also compares all remaining payments on both loans. Rent vs buy finds the home price where PITI minus the monthly tax savings equals the rent. Tax savings assume you itemize; most households take the standard deduction, so set the bracket to 0 if that's you. Not tax advice.

What investment property figures are shown?

Net operating income (NOI) = rent and other income × 12, less vacancy, less operating expenses. Cap rate = NOI ÷ price. Cash flow = NOI − 12 × principal and interest. Cash-on-cash = cash flow ÷ cash invested (down payment, closing costs and repairs). DSCR = NOI ÷ yearly debt service.

How was the math tested?

Against 58 published worked examples (167 values): the user's guide of a widely used handheld real estate finance calculator, the CFPB sample Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure, and published cap rate, DSCR, cash-on-cash and break-even examples. Another 184 formula cases are generated from first principles by an independent reference implementation.
